## Day 1 · Jul 4 — Arrival in Santa Cristina Val Gardena (German: St. Christina in Gröden)

Santa Cristina Val Gardena (German: St. Christina in Gröden) is one of the three villages of Val Gardena (German: Gröden), alongside Ortisei (German: St. Ulrich) and Selva (German: Wolkenstein). From the hotel, the view opens straight up the valley toward Sassolungo (German: Langkofel, 3,181m) and its neighbour Sassopiatto (German: Plattkofel, 2,957m) — the same peaks we'd be hiking around the next day.

## Day 2 · Jul 5 — Sassolungo Circuit & the "Sarglift"

**Route:** Forcella Sassolungo loop from Passo Sella (German: Sellajoch)

The Forcella del Sassolungo cable car — locally nicknamed the *Sarglift* ("coffin lift") in German — is one of the Dolomites' most talked-about rides: narrow, upright two-person cabins that don't stop to let you board. What began in 1959 as an open standing basket was rebuilt into today's coffin-shaped pods, climbing from Passo Sella up to the Langkofelscharte (2,685m), right at the foot of the Cinque Dita (Five Fingers) rock towers.

Stepping off the cable car, the landscape changes completely — bare rock and lingering snow even in early July, hemmed in by jagged spires. A new via ferrata, Furcela de Saslonch, opened here in 2022, tracing the ridge right beside the pass — it's clearly a serious climbing area, not just a viewpoint. We looped back down on foot, past Rifugio Vicenza and onto gentler, grassier terrain scattered with smaller boulders — a stretch locally called the Steinerne Stadt ("Stone City") — before returning to Passo Sella. If you're feeling tired partway through, there's also the option of a chairlift back down to Santa Cristina.

## Day 3 · Jul 6 — Via Ferrata Sas Rigais

**Route:** Via Ferrata Sas Rigais (German: Klettersteig-Überschreitung) · Grade A/B

Sas Rigais — 3,025m, the highest peak of the Geisler group (Italian/Ladin: Odle) — is where a young Reinhold Messner first climbed with his father in 1949; the Geisler group is still sometimes called his backyard. From the Col Raiser cable car, the route climbs past Rifugio Firenze (German: Regensburger Hütte) with views back toward the Geisler/Odle peaks, then up the fixed cables to the summit, and down over the Mesdì Pass with more views across to the Odle spires and the wider Dolomites.

Since it was my friend's first time on a via ferrata, I chose an A/B-grade route. Even at that grade, it demanded solid fitness — a long, gentle approach comes first, well before the climbing itself even begins. Our guide's alternative suggestion, if we'd wanted something shorter, was Via Ferrata Pisciadù near Passo Gardena — graded B/C, but much shorter, and by all accounts just as scenic. We ended up slower than planned and missed the last cable car down, so we walked out instead.

## Day 4 · Jul 7 — Seceda

**Route:** Seceda from Col Raiser

Seceda is arguably the most photographed mountain in the Dolomites — jagged Geisler/Odle spires on one side, soft green pasture falling toward Val Gardena (German: Gröden) on the other. The image everyone recognises isn't visible right at the cable car exit, though; it takes a short walk out along the ridge to a specific viewpoint to line it up.

We came up on the same Col Raiser cable car as the day before, and could already see Sas Rigais and the ridge from that first angle — just not yet Seceda's famous face. Between the late start and a grey, closed-in sky, we never made it to the viewpoint that shows Seceda at its best.

## Day 5 · Jul 8 — Dantercëpies & the Crespëina Plateau

**Route:** Dantercëpies (German: Grödnerjoch/Passo Gardena) toward Lech de Crespëina

From Selva (German: Wolkenstein), the two-stage Dantercepies I/II cable car climbs to Passo Gardena (German: Grödnerjoch), gateway to the Puez-Odle plateau — a landscape locals compare to walking on the moon. There are two ways back down: the long way, trail 2, crosses the whole plateau — past Jimmy's Hut (Rifugio Jimmi), over Forcella Cir, to Forcella/Passo Crespëina and Lech de Crespëina, on to Rifugio Puez, then down via trail 14. The short way turns back at Jimmy's Hut onto trail 12A, connecting to trail 12 for a quicker descent.

After three demanding days, we planned to shorten our route partway through — but the paper map we were reading was more illustration than accurate topography, and in the end we stayed on trail 2 the whole way out, no shortcut taken. By the time we reached Lech de Crespëina (2,374m), an unmarked, cairned path continuing beyond the lake looked steep, and with no one else in sight across the open pasture, we turned back rather than push on. By the time we retraced our way to the Dantercëpies station, the cable car had already closed for the day — so we ended up walking all the way down anyway, following what turned out to be the first stretch of trail 12A, the same corridor used by the shorter loop option.
